Watching the lynx, lightly

An AI camera trap for Eurasian lynx that is accurate, frugal with energy, and affordable.

Roham Koohestani & Nina Semjanová
TU Delft, CS4800, September 2026

The case

An elusive cat, a forest of cameras

About 9,000 lynx live in Europe, in 11 mostly stable populations .

Researchers follow individual cats with camera traps, over long sessions and many seasons .

An AI that sorts them is pulled three ways:

  • Accuracy: find the lynx
  • Energy: batteries in a remote forest
  • Cost: conservation budgets

So we make three decisions: what data, how to train, and where to run it.

Part 1.1: Training data

What the model learns from

Our core dataset is CzechLynx: 39,760 images of 319 known individuals over 15 years .

Every image has a pose skeleton and a segmentation mask, plus synthetic images to fill the gaps .

One forest is not enough, so we add DeepFaune, 2M+ European images .

And we add empty frames and other species: about 76% of Snapshot Serengeti is empty .

Models that ace a familiar site often fail at a new one . So we split by location: we always test on unseen sites.

Part 1.2: Training strategy

Small models, modest GPUs

We start small. EfficientNet-B0 has 5x fewer parameters than ResNet-50 .

It needs about 10x less compute per image, at the same accuracy .

Wildlife detectors show the same thing: the smaller MegaDetector finds more animals than the big one .

So one modest GPU is enough: a T4 costs $0.16 to $0.21 per hour on spot instances .

Part 1.3: Deployment strategy

Thinking at the edge

In the cloud, every trigger means uploading a full photo from the middle of a forest.

We run the model on the camera and send a 44-byte message instead.

Small accelerators make that possible: a Coral draws 2 W and costs $60 to $75 .

The bar to beat: a plain trap on 12 AA batteries .

The radio matters too. For small messages, LoRaWAN uses about 18x less energy than NB-IoT .

Already in the field

On-device detection already works in the wild.

TrailGuard AI

On-device Movidius VPU detection, alerts in under 30 seconds, about 1.5 years of idle battery life .

Instant Detect 2.0

LoRa camera and sensor nodes with an Iridium satellite base station, about 3,600 images per month .

Sentinel

On-device detection that uploads compressed detections over satellite, cellular, LoRa or WiFi .

Gabon, 2023

The closest peer-reviewed case: a Raspberry Pi with TensorFlow Lite and Iridium alerts in low-connectivity tropical forest, with notifications within minutes .

Vendor figures are the vendors' own claims and have not been independently verified.
